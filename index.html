How it works

Every Plumble is a closed skin of 10,242 particles, each tied to its spot in the rest shape by a damped spring. Firmness sets how hard the skin pushes back: soft ones squash flat to the cloth, firm ones barely give. Slow rise sets the damping, from jelly bounce to memory-foam creep.

Pieces are solid to each other: stretch or shove one into another and the one you hit dents, pushes back, and slides away if you push hard enough.

The knife chops straight down along the line you swipe, and each piece is rebuilt as its own Plumble with a real cut face. The rolling pin presses from above, so the more you roll, the flatter it spreads. Every sound is made on the spot, so no two squishes sound quite the same.
